Lemonade has [idiopathic epilepsy](/conditions/idiopathic-epilepsy/). He usually shows clear warning signs 18–24 hours before a seizure. Every seizure so far has started while he was asleep.

> [!important]
> **Plan (agreed 26 October 2026):** no daily medication. Start levetiracetam as soon as the warning signs appear (step 2).

## 1. Recognise the warning signs

Usually at night, about 18–24 hours before a seizure:

- Extremely nervous; wakes people up; jumps on the bed
- Won't settle for 3–4 hours
- Stares up at the ceiling; panting
- Noise-sensitive (especially wind)
- Seems to want to go outside, but is still stressed out there

He usually settles and seems normal the next day. The seizure tends to come after that.

## 2. Start levetiracetam

- Give **1 tablet (1,000 mg) every 8 hours for 5 days**, starting straight away
- After the first tablet, move the doses onto 07:00, 15:00 and 23:00
- Give it even if you're not sure; a false alarm does no harm
- Tell Lewis the date and time it was started

## 3. Keep things calm for 24 hours

- Blinds closed, lights low, TV and music off
- Minimise wind noise (doors taped, draught stoppers)
- Toilet trips only; no big walks or excitement
- Keep rescue diazepam within reach

## 4. During a seizure

- **Note the time it starts** (start a timer on your phone). Film it if you can
- Move anything that could hurt him, **only if it is safe to**
- On a hard floor, slide something soft under his head if it is safe
- **Keep hands away from his mouth.** Jaw chattering causes bites
- Turn the lights off, then leave the room and close the door. He can be confused and defensive afterwards

> [!warning]
> **Still seizing at 3 minutes? Give rectal diazepam straight away.** Don't wait to see if it stops on its own.
>
> If he is still seizing 1–2 minutes after the diazepam, give a second dose. Then take him straight to the emergency vet: **Vets Now Manchester, 0161 222 6101**.

## 5. After a seizure

- He usually barks and panics for 10–20 minutes; give him space
- Check in every 10–15 minutes and speak softly; he suddenly recognises you and becomes excited
- He will drink a lot and may need a wee
- Ice cream is fine as a treat (no medical effect)

## When to go to the emergency vet

- A seizure lasting more than 3 minutes (give the diazepam first, then go)
- A second seizure within 24 hours
- He doesn't recover normally between seizures
- New problems that persist, such as weakness, blindness or a behaviour change

## Good to know

- On levetiracetam he is calmer than usual (relaxed in the car, ignores wind). Mild wobbliness can happen after a large dose
- Aprylic MCT oil continues every day regardless
- If prodrome-triggered levetiracetam doesn't control things over the following months, the next step is daily phenobarbital
