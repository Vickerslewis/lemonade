## Current approach

Per specialist advice — **keep active**. Movement is one of the best things for arthritis.

> "If he's doing good, I think it is good to do more, because basically doing more is the best thing for his arthritis anyway."

> [!tip]
> On the day of a seizure or warning signs, follow the [action plan](/action-plan/) instead: toilet trips only, no big walks or excitement.

## Daily exercise

- 3–4 walks a day (currently on lead)
- Build up gradually as tolerated
- If he is keen and not getting worse, do more
- Any deterioration: rest for a day or two, then resume

## Surfaces

- **Grass** — preferred; runs and plays freely
- **Pavement / concrete** — slower, more uncomfortable

> [!tip]
> Use the boot on his left foot for pavement walks. It isn't needed on grass.

## Recommended

- **Swimming** — strongly recommended; non-weight-bearing and good for joint mobility
- **Grass walks** — encouraged; off-lead where safe
- **Hiking** — likely fine if built up gradually and not too long on hard ground
- **Play** — fine; watch for jarring or twisting on hard surfaces

## Avoid or moderate

- Sudden jarring movements (suspected cause of the April 2026 flare)
- Long pavement walks without the boot
- Slippery floors (risk of twisting injuries)
- Jumping from heights

## Observations

- He often slows on the way home — could be arthritis (overdoing it) or reluctance to go home; watch other signs to tell them apart
- Adrenaline and excitement can mask discomfort — don't rely on him to self-limit
- The first few minutes of a walk can be stiff, then it improves

## Long-term

- Muscle mass and cardiovascular fitness protect against arthritis progression
- Weight management is critical — keep him lean (see [diet](/care/diet-and-supplements/))
