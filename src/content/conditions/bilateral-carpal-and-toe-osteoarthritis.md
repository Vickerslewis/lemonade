## Summary

Chronic bilateral osteoarthritis affecting the knuckle (metacarpophalangeal) joints, most severely the **left fifth toe**. Likely longstanding. A trip in approximately mid-April 2026 made it worse, triggering the lameness that prompted investigation. The carpus and elbows are both normal on CT.

## Diagnostic findings

- **CT, both forelimbs (12 May 2026)**, plus examination under anaesthesia
- Proliferative periosteal reaction on the distal left 5th metacarpal bone and proximal phalanx
- Periarticular mineralisation and moderate joint capsule thickening in the left 5th MCP joint
- Milder MCP osteoarthritis in several joints: right foot (5th and 2nd); left foot (2nd and 5th)
- No soft tissue mass; no lytic lesions — cancer ruled out
- No instability on examination (conscious or anaesthetised) — ligaments intact
- Carpus normal on CT (the earlier X-ray opacity was in the MCP region, not the carpus)
- Elbows normal — no dysplasia, no arthritis
- See [imaging](/imaging/)

## Why it is not cancer

- Bone tumours destroy bone (lytic changes); Lemonade's changes are additive (new bone forming)
- Bone tumours typically affect one side of a joint; Lemonade has changes on both sides
- Bilateral, symmetric distribution is inconsistent with a primary bone tumour

## Clinical signs

- Slower on hard surfaces (pavement, concrete)
- Runs and plays freely on grass
- Worse on the way back from long walks
- Improves significantly with rest
- Range of motion preserved on examination
- Currently sound at walk

## Management

### Medication

- **None currently.** Paracetamol was given for a few weeks only; Onsior is not being given (confirmed by owner, 26 October 2026). See [medications](/medications/)
- Arthritis has caused no problems since May 2026

### Physical

- **Acupuncture** — weekly (started 18 June 2026); he sleeps through it
- **Boot** for the left (affected) foot — protection and more pain-free use on hard ground
- **K-laser therapy with a physiotherapist** — next step if needed
- **Swimming** — encouraged for fitness and joint mobility
- **Activity** — gradually back to normal while he uses the limb well; sustained activity helps arthritis long-term

See [physiotherapy and rehab](/care/physiotherapy-and-rehab/) and [exercise](/care/exercise-and-activity/).

### Diet and supplements

- Omega-3 fatty acids — moderate evidence for benefit in canine osteoarthritis
- Joint supplement (glucosamine/chondroitin) or a prescription mobility diet — discussed as a future option
- See [diet and supplements](/care/diet-and-supplements/)

### Reserved options

- NSAIDs (Onsior or similar) and paracetamol — if symptoms flare
- Intra-articular injections (steroid, hyaluronic acid or PRP)
- Surgery (joint fusion or toe amputation) — only if conservative treatment fails
- Gabapentin — if a nerve-pain component develops

## Prognosis

Progressive, but the rate of change is unpredictable. It may plateau and stay stable for years if there are no further injuries. Currently managed without medication; NSAIDs and paracetamol can be used long-term with monitoring if needed. Not curable, but very manageable — a long, comfortable life is expected with proper management.
