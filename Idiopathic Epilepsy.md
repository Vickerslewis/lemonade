# Idiopathic Epilepsy

> Confirmed idiopathic epilepsy; no structural or metabolic cause found. Five seizures to date, including a cluster on 22 October 2026. Treatment was recommended at the 26 October 2026 neurology consultation; the chosen plan is levetiracetam whenever the prodrome starts.

## Summary

Idiopathic epilepsy was confirmed after a brain MRI in May 2026 and re-confirmed by Northwest Veterinary Specialists on 26 October 2026. No structural brain abnormality, tumour, or metabolic cause has been found. Lemonade has had five seizures in about 11 months. The two most recent formed a cluster (two within 24 hours) on 22 October 2026, and because of that cluster the neurologist now recommends treatment. He is unusual in having a clear, reliable prodrome 18–24 hours before a seizure.

## Diagnostic Findings

- **Date:** 2026-05
- **Modality:** Brain MRI
- **Key findings:**
  - No tumours
  - Structurally normal brain
  - Mild increase of spinal fluid (CSF) around the midbrain/brainstem. Probably congenital; causes no clinical problems; in the wrong location to explain seizures (re-reviewed 2026-10-26)
  - No hydrocephalus or other concerning findings
- **Bloods:** normal after the first seizure (2025-11) and on a small panel at the emergency vet (2026-10-22). Baseline haematology and biochemistry taken 2026-10-26 — results {{TBD}}
- **Conclusion:** Structural and metabolic causes excluded; idiopathic epilepsy criteria met
- See [[Imaging History]] for full imaging record

## Clinical Signs

Seizure characteristics (based on 5 recorded events — see [[Seizure Log]] for full detail):

- All five seizures were generalised tonic-clonic (whole body)
- Duration 50 seconds to ~1 minute 20 seconds
- All occurred during sleep or at night
- Onset: while asleep he slowly lifts his head at an odd angle, looks up at the ceiling, then seizes
- Rarely urinates; never defecates; mild salivation afterwards
- Seizures 4 and 5 were a cluster, 5 h 47 min apart

Known pre-ictal pattern (prodrome):
- About 18–24 hours before, usually at night: extremely nervous, wakes people up, won't settle for 3–4 hours, stares at the ceiling, pants, noise-sensitive (especially wind)
- Seen before seizures 1, 2 and 4
- Several prodrome-like nights in 2026 were not followed by a seizure (low-stimulus environment kept for 24 hours)

Post-ictal pattern:
- Episodes 1 and 2: aggressive barking and biting; did not recognise owner; resolved within ~20–40 minutes
- Episode 3: calm on waking; no aggression
- Episode 4: barking; settled in ~15 minutes
- Episode 5: lay on his side without moving for ~30–45 seconds before getting up; erratic, then calm within 10–15 minutes

## Management

### Medications

- **Plan (Option 2, chosen 2026-10-26):** no daily medication. Levetiracetam 500 mg every 8 hours for 5 days whenever the prodrome is recognised. Follow [[Seizure Action Plan]]
- Post-cluster levetiracetam course (2026-10-22 to 2026-10-26) finished — see [[Medications]]
- **Alternative / next step (Option 1):** daily phenobarbital with regular blood monitoring
- **Rescue:** rectal diazepam if a seizure passes 3 minutes

### Diet and Supplements

- MCT oil daily since the first seizure; the neurologist endorsed it — see [[Diet and Supplements]]
- No evidence for avoiding specific proteins or gluten

### Monitoring

- Record every prodrome and every seizure in [[Seizure Log]], including when levetiracetam was started
- Contact Northwest after any seizure
- Recheck at Northwest within 6 months to stay under specialist care

Environmental management during and after a seizure:
- Keep the environment quiet
- Dim or turn off lights
- Close windows to reduce external noise
- Do not restrain — observe from a safe distance
- Supervise walks until fully recovered

> [!warning]
> **Seek emergency vet care immediately if:**
> - Any single seizure lasts longer than 3 minutes (give rectal diazepam first)
> - Two or more seizures occur within 24 hours
> - He does not recover normally between seizures
> - New neurological signs (weakness, blindness, behaviour change) persist

## Prognosis

Idiopathic epilepsy is managed, not cured; the aim is fewer seizures rather than none. About 70% of dogs respond to medication. Some become drug-resistant and need several drugs. Lemonade's starting point (five seizures in ~11 months) is good, and the neurologist saw nothing to suggest he will be drug-resistant. It is likely genetic. A very small number of epileptic dogs die suddenly for unknown reasons (SUDEP); this is rare.
