# Exercise and Activity

> Activity guidance following the osteoarthritis diagnosis; movement is therapeutic, not merely exercise.

## Current Approach

Per specialist advice — **keep active**. Movement is one of the best things for arthritis.

> "If he's doing good, I think it is good to do more, because basically doing more is the best thing for his arthritis anyway."

## Guidelines

### Daily Exercise
- 3–4 walks per day (currently on lead)
- Build up gradually as tolerated
- If he is keen and not deteriorating → do more
- Any deterioration → rest for a day or two, then resume

### Surface Preferences
- **Grass** — preferred; runs and plays freely
- **Pavement/concrete** — slower, more uncomfortable

> [!tip]
> Use padded boots for pavement walks on both forepaws (for balance and symmetry). Cheap velcro-strap options are sufficient at this stage. See [[Physiotherapy and Rehab]].

### Recommended Activities
- **Swimming** — strongly recommended. Non-weight-bearing; good for joint mobility.
- **Grass walks** — encouraged; off-lead where safe
- **Hiking** — likely fine if built up gradually and not extended on hard ground
- **Play** — fine; monitor for jarring or twisting on hard surfaces

### Activities to Avoid or Moderate
- Sudden jarring movements (suspected cause of the most recent flare)
- Long pavement walks without boots
- Slippery floors (risk of twisting injuries)
- Jumping from heights

## Observations

- He often slows on the way home — may be arthritis (overdoing it) or reluctance to return home; monitor other signs to distinguish
- Adrenaline and excitement can mask discomfort — do not rely on him to self-limit
- A warm-up period matters — the first few minutes of a walk can be stiff, then improves

## Long-term

- Maintaining muscle mass and cardiovascular fitness is protective against arthritis progression
- Weight management is critical — keep him lean (see [[Diet and Supplements]])
- Activity is a treatment, not just exercise
