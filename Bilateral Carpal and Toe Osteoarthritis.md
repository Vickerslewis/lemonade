# Bilateral Carpal and Toe Osteoarthritis

> Chronic bilateral metacarpophalangeal osteoarthritis, most severely the left fifth toe; likely longstanding, exacerbated by a trip in approximately mid-April 2026.

## Summary

Chronic bilateral osteoarthritis affecting the knuckle (metacarpophalangeal) joints, most severely the **left fifth toe**. Likely longstanding. A trip in approximately mid-April 2026 exacerbated the pre-existing condition, triggering lameness that prompted investigation. The carpus and elbows are both normal on CT.

## Diagnostic Findings

- **Date:** 2026-05-12
- **Modality:** CT (both forelimbs); physical examination under anaesthesia
- **Key findings:**
  - Proliferative periosteal reaction on the distal left 5th metacarpal bone and proximal phalanx
  - Periarticular mineralisation and moderate joint capsule thickening in the left 5th MCP joint
  - Milder MCP osteoarthritis in multiple joints: right foot (5th and 2nd joints); left foot (2nd and 5th joints)
  - No soft tissue mass; no lytic lesions — cancer ruled out
  - No instability on examination (conscious or anaesthetised) — ligaments intact
  - Carpus: normal on CT (previous X-ray opacity was in the MCP region, not the carpus)
  - Elbows: normal — no dysplasia, no arthritis
- See [[Imaging History]] for full imaging record

## Why It is Not Cancer

- Bone tumours destroy bone (lytic changes). Lemonade's changes are additive (new bone forming).
- Bone tumours typically affect one side of a joint. Lemonade has changes on both sides of the affected joints.
- Bilateral, symmetric distribution is inconsistent with a primary bone tumour.

## Clinical Signs

- Slower on hard surfaces (pavement, concrete)
- Runs and plays freely on grass
- Worse on the return from long walks
- Improves significantly with rest
- Range of motion preserved on examination
- Currently sound at walk

## Management

See [[Medications]] for the full current drug regimen and daily schedule.

### Medications
- **Onsior (robenacoxib)** — daily NSAID, ongoing
- **Paracetamol** — 500 mg, 3× daily, added on top of Onsior
- Both can be safely given together; long-term use is safe with periodic blood monitoring

### Physical
- **Boot** for the left (affected) foot — provides protection and allows more pain-free use on hard ground
- **Acupuncture** — booked; 5 sessions starting 2026-06-18 (see [[Physiotherapy and Rehab]])
- **K-laser therapy with physiotherapist** — next step after acupuncture if needed (see [[Physiotherapy and Rehab]])
- **Swimming** — encouraged for general fitness and joint mobility (see [[Exercise and Activity]])
- **Activity** — gradually increase exercise back to normal while using limb well; sustained activity is beneficial for arthritis long-term

### Diet and Supplements
- Omega-3 fatty acids — moderate evidence for benefit in canine OA
- Joint supplement (glucosamine/chondroitin) or prescription mobility diet — discussed as future option
- See [[Diet and Supplements]] for current regimen and details

### Reserved Options
- Intra-articular injections (steroid, hyaluronic acid, or PRP)
- Surgical options (joint fusion or toe amputation) — only if conservative treatment fails
- Gabapentin — if a nerve pain component develops

## Prognosis

Progressive condition, but rate of change is unpredictable. May plateau and remain stable for years if no further injuries. NSAIDs and paracetamol can be continued long-term with monitoring. Not curative, but very manageable. A long, comfortable life is expected with proper management.
