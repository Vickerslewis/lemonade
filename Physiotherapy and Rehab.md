# Physiotherapy and Rehab

> Physiotherapy and rehabilitation for managing [[Bilateral Carpal and Toe Osteoarthritis]]. Treatment plan: acupuncture first (booked), then K-laser if needed.

## Current Plan

**Step 1 — Acupuncture:** Booked for 2026-06-18. 5 sessions planned.

**Step 2 — K-Laser Therapy:** Next step if acupuncture does not achieve sufficient improvement.

## Active Interventions

### Acupuncture
- **Status:** Booked — first session 2026-06-18; 5 sessions planned
- **What:** Dry needling to reduce pain and inflammation; may also have systemic effects on chronic pain
- **Why:** Recommended by Nick Macdonald (NW Vet Specialists) as first-line physical intervention
- **Response:** Some dogs respond well; others do not — 5 sessions is a reasonable trial period

### Boot (Left Foot)
- For pavement/hard-surface walks — left foot only (the affected side)
- Provides protection and allows more pain-free use on hard ground
- Not necessary on grass
- See [[Exercise and Activity]]

### Swimming
- Non-weight-bearing exercise
- Good for general joint mobility
- Recommended by specialist
- See [[Exercise and Activity]]

## Next Step

### K-Laser Therapy with Physiotherapist
- **What:** Class IV laser therapy — promotes soft tissue healing and reduces inflammation
- **When:** If acupuncture does not resolve current inflammation sufficiently
- **Frequency:** As advised by physiotherapist
- **Comfort:** Not painful; may feel slightly warm. Most dogs tolerate it well.
- **How to access:** Ask Companion Care for a local physiotherapist referral, or attend NW Vet Specialists if they have a laser facility

## Reserved / Future Options

### Intra-articular Injections
- Steroid, hyaluronic acid, or stem cell/PRP options
- Not currently indicated
- To be considered if conservative treatment fails
