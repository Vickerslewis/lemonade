# Physiotherapy and Rehab

> Physiotherapy and rehabilitation options for managing [[Bilateral Carpal and Toe Osteoarthritis]]; K-laser therapy has been recommended by the specialist as the primary intervention.

## Current Approach

K-laser therapy with a physiotherapist has been recommended by the specialist. Not yet started — to be arranged via the local vet.

## Recommended Interventions

### K-Laser Therapy with Physiotherapist
- **What:** Class IV laser therapy — promotes soft tissue healing and reduces inflammation
- **Why:** Targeted treatment for the affected toe joints; specifically recommended by the specialist
- **Frequency:** As advised by physiotherapist
- **Comfort:** Not painful; may feel slightly warm. Most dogs tolerate it well.
- **How to access:**
  - Specialist will write to local vet with recommendation
  - Local vet may have a physiotherapist they regularly refer to
  - Alternative: travel to specialist's centre if they have a laser facility locally

### Padded Boots
- For pavement walks
- Both forepaws (for balance and symmetry)
- Cheap velcro-strap options are sufficient — likely temporary
- Not necessary on grass
- See [[Exercise and Activity]]

### Swimming
- Non-weight-bearing exercise
- Good for general joint mobility (especially elbows, hips, and shoulders)
- Less direct benefit for toe joints specifically, but no downside
- See [[Exercise and Activity]]

## Reserved / Future Options

### Acupuncture
- Some dogs respond well; others do not
- No significant downside
- Specialist considers Lemonade likely to be amenable to it
- Held in reserve — to be considered after painkillers, boots, and laser have been trialled
- Previously available at specialist's clinic but practitioner has moved; a local provider would need to be found

### Intra-articular Injections
- Steroid, hyaluronic acid, or stem cell/PRP options
- Not currently indicated
- To be considered if conservative treatment fails
