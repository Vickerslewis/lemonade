# Seizure Action Plan

> What to do before, during and after a seizure. Written for anyone looking after Lemonade. Based on advice from Northwest Veterinary Specialists, 26 October 2026 — see [[Vet Visits]].

## Current Approach

Lemonade has [[Idiopathic Epilepsy]]. He usually shows clear warning signs (a prodrome) 18–24 hours before a seizure. Every seizure so far has started while he was asleep.

> [!important]
> **Plan (agreed 26 October 2026):** no daily medication. Start levetiracetam as soon as the warning signs appear (Step 2).

## Guidelines

### Step 1 — Recognise the Warning Signs

Usually at night, about 18–24 hours before a seizure:

- Extremely nervous; wakes people up; jumps on the bed
- Won't settle for 3–4 hours
- Stares up at the ceiling; panting
- Noise-sensitive (especially wind)
- Seems to want to go outside, but is still stressed out there

He usually settles and seems normal the next day. The seizure tends to come after that.

### Step 2 — Start Levetiracetam

- Give **1 tablet (500 mg) every 8 hours for 5 days**, starting straight away
- Then carry on at 07:00, 15:00 and 23:00
- Give it even if you're not sure; a false alarm does no harm
- Log the date and time it was started in [[Seizure Log]]

### Step 3 — Keep Things Calm for 24 Hours

- Blinds closed, lights low, TV and music off
- Minimise wind noise (doors taped, draught stoppers)
- Toilet trips only; no big walks or excitement
- Keep rescue diazepam within reach

### Step 4 — During a Seizure

- **Note the time it starts.** Film it if you can
- Move anything that could hurt him, **only if it is safe to**
- On a hard floor, slide something soft under his head if it is safe
- **Keep hands away from his mouth.** Jaw chattering causes bites
- Turn the lights off, then leave the room and close the door. He can be confused and defensive afterwards

> [!important]
> **If the seizure passes 3 minutes:** give rectal diazepam. If there is no response within 1–2 minutes, a second dose can be given. Then go to the emergency vet.

### Step 5 — After a Seizure

- He usually barks and panics for 10–20 minutes; give him space
- Check in every 10–15 minutes and speak softly; he suddenly recognises you and becomes excited
- He will drink a lot and may need a wee
- Ice cream is fine as a treat (no medical effect)

### When to Go to the Emergency Vet

- A seizure lasting more than 3 minutes (after giving diazepam)
- A second seizure within 24 hours
- He doesn't recover normally between seizures
- New problems that persist, such as weakness, blindness or a behaviour change

### Who to Tell

- **Owner (Lewis Vickers):** straight away, for any prodrome or seizure
- **Northwest Veterinary Specialists:** after **any** seizure, even a short one (owner will contact them)
- **Emergency vet:** Vets Now — Manchester Hospital, 98 Bury Old Road, Whitefield, Manchester, M45 6TQ. Open 24/7

## Observations

- On levetiracetam he is calmer than usual (relaxed in the car, ignores wind). Mild wobbliness can happen after a large dose
- Aprylic MCT oil continues every day regardless — see [[Diet and Supplements]]

## Long-term / Future Options

- Contact Northwest after any seizure. If prodrome-triggered levetiracetam doesn't control things over the following months, the next step is daily phenobarbital — see [[Medications]]
- Recheck at Northwest within 6 months to stay under their care
